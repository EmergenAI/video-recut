# Playbooks

Read this when the creation and execution references leave a directing question open: how a kind of
video usually works, or how to handle one recurring craft problem inside it.

The playbooks inform judgment. They never hold the facts of the work itself: those stay in the
composition page, `script.json`, the prompt files and the generation requests.

## Two scales of advice

- **Formats** describe a whole work: who carries the argument, how attention travels, and what the
  piece reveals, pays off or ends on.
- **Craft pages** describe one local problem that turns up across formats: captions, B-roll,
  continuity between generated shots, image prompts, sound, graphic layout, and so on.

Let the material at hand choose the pages: the analysis and timeline of the reference, the Brief and
Treatment for the target, and the composition as it stands. One work can borrow from several formats and several craft
pages at once. A new or hybrid form may need craft pages only, with no format that matches. When no
page fits, direct from the project's own material (analysis, reference timeline, Brief, Treatment,
composition, draft renders). A work never has to be classified before it can be made.

## Craft pages

Read the craft page for every problem the work actually has, including problems a draft render or an
evidence frame has just exposed. If you are reusing the same craft on another passage and nothing
about the decision has changed, you do not need to read it again.

| Page | Read it when |
| --- | --- |
| [Image direction](../3-materials/images.md) | You need a picture of a person, place, product or icon; you are after phone-video texture, casting, the camera's relation to the subject, or reference images |
| [Voice direction](../3-materials/voices.md) | You are choosing or designing a voice: what makes it appealing, its character, how to describe it, which sample line to use |
| [Voice and performance](../3-materials/speakers-and-narration.md) | You are deciding which performance carries a passage, how a recurring voice identity is kept, how one piece of footage serves several pictures, or whether narration is truly independent |
| [Video direction](../3-materials/video.md) | You are choosing a generation relationship, deciding when the source material should carry action or camera movement, directing visible speech or silent action, cutting, requesting subject cut-outs, or setting request duration |
| [Generated dependencies](../3-materials/reference-chains.md) | An existing image or clip must feed a new request: a person, a place, a conversational angle, a prop that continues, or a new scene branching off |
| [B-roll](../4-compose/supporting-footage.md) | A picture or clip contributes to a passage: full screen or framed, exact evidence or visual idea, and how it hands off with the performance |
| [Screen demonstrations](../4-compose/screen-demos.md) | A website, app, terminal or editor must be understood: recorded state and interaction versus an authored explanation, and how the viewer keeps up |
| [Captions](../4-compose/captions-style.md) | Text bound to speech: reading rhythm, style, or a caption structure you have not built before |
| [Caption tracking](../4-compose/captions-follow.md) | Only when the reference clearly uses head-following captions or the user asks: face observations per speaker, placement, Cue timing |
| [Sound mix](../4-compose/mix.md) | You are deciding what the footage already sounds like, what music or effects it needs, and how sounds share time and attention |
| [Graphic compositions](../4-compose/graphics-layout.md) | Footage and graphics must share the frame: attention, framing of the source, boards, spatial hierarchy, a coordinated palette |
| [Motion graphics](../4-compose/motion.md) | Graphics, effects or a scene change over time: objects that persist through a handoff, a motion language, timing against the performance |

## Formats

A format page helps only when its relationships shed light on this work. It is not a category you
must declare, and its examples do not limit how you combine or invent.

| Page | The shape it describes |
| --- | --- |
| [Talking head](talking-head.md) | UGC or direct-to-camera: one person speaking to the viewer, performance cut naturally |
| [Short drama](short-drama.md) | Dramatic scenes in rich A-roll, built on characters, their relationships, what they do and how things change |
| [Two-person podcast](podcast.md) | Two hosts in matching angles, reactions within the conversation, handling a product, or everyday cover shots |
| [Street interview](street-interview.md) | Guest and interviewer meeting for a reason, seen in shared views, with revealing or tracking captions |
| [Ranking listicle](ranking.md) | A comparison or board that stays on screen and changes state as the argument moves |
| [Presenter-led explainer](presenter-explainer.md) | A presenter combined with demonstrations and motion graphics, moving between full screen, inset and covered |
| [Narration-led demo](narration-demo.md) | Independent narration organizing product shots, hands, screens, demonstrations, montage or motion graphics |

## Turning a decision into production

Once a playbook has settled a role or a relationship, write it down as facts production can act on.

- The [execution index](../4-compose/overview.md) says where each fact lives: script, timeline,
  composition page, prompt files, generated media.
- [Generation](../3-materials/generation-requests.md) holds the exact Bibei requests, models and limits, and any
  prompt wording that has been proven.
- [Services](../1-setup/services.md) holds what the Bibei account and the chosen TTS service can
  really do.
- The creation references hold the understanding and intent of the work; the project files hold its
  current facts and decisions.
